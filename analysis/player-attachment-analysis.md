# Player Attachment — Which Managers Keep Coming Back to the Same Players

How repeatedly each manager rosters the same player. Built from 12,160 weekly roster records (2022–2025, 17 weeks a season) and 1,400 draft picks (2018–2025).

## What is measured

- **Weeks** — total weeks that manager had that player on their roster. The ceiling is 68 (4 seasons × 17 weeks).
- **Seasons** — how many different seasons they rostered him.
- **Share** — of every week that player spent on *anyone's* roster in this league, the percentage that was this manager. 100% means nobody else ever had him.
- **Stints** — separate unbroken spells. More stints than seasons means they let him go and brought him back.
- **Repeat drafts** — how many separate drafts they spent a pick on him, going back to 2018.

Weekly roster data covers 2022–2025. Draft data goes back to 2018, so the repeat-draft section reaches further than the rest.

## Caveats

- Weekly roster data starts in 2022, so a bond that formed earlier only counts from then. The repeat-draft table is the one place earlier seasons show up.
- Share is measured against weeks *rostered in this league*, not NFL availability. A player who was mostly a free agent can reach a high share on few weeks — the weeks column is the check on that.
- Attachment is not the same as good process: a manager can keep re-rostering a player because he keeps being available on waivers, not because they are chasing him.
- "Jeff Chan" in the 2018 draft data is the same person as Jeffrey Chan and is merged. Daniel Ota (2019–20) appears only in draft data and has no weekly roster history here.
