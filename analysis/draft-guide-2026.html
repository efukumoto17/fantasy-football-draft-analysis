Two things to understand before you start

1. This is a two-quarterback league. Quarterbacks are worth far more here than the ESPN rankings on your screen suggest — most QBs on our board sit 40 to 95 spots above where the market drafts them. That gap is the edge in this draft. But it's a trap early: a quarterback will look like the best player available at nearly every pick from Round 2 on. The plan starts the quarterbacks in Round 5, and they will still be there. Trust that.

2. Players carry tags. Names marked Favorite or Huge Upside Sleeper Value are the ones Evan wants — prefer them when the choice is close. Names marked Do Not Draft are exactly that, no matter how good the value looks; the full list is in section 04. Injured is a flag, not a veto.

05If the draft goes sideways

Drafts don't follow scripts. Find the situation that matches and follow it — each of these is a real adjustment, not a panic move.

If · we reach Round 7 with only one running back

Take running backs in Rounds 7 and 8

The most likely way to go wrong, because receivers keep looking like better value. Skip the Round 7 receiver and take the best tagged back available.

  • Bhayshul Tuten — Huge Upside, on the board almost always
  • MarShawn Lloyd — Huge Upside; he also lasts to Round 10, so prefer Tuten first
  • J.K. Dobbins — Favorite + Value, available into Round 11

Receiver is the deepest position on this board — fifteen-plus receivers still carry a positive gap in Rounds 9 through 13. Running back dries up first. When in doubt in the middle rounds, take the back.

If · a quarterback run starts before Round 5

Move the QBs up one round, don't chase further

If four or more quarterbacks go before our Round 5 pick, take the first one there and the second in Round 6 as planned. Do not reach into Round 3 or 4 to get ahead of it.

Nine quarterbacks carry a +50 gap or better. Even in the worst case we get two. The names change; the value doesn't.

  • Deep fallbacks, nearly always available past Round 6: Brock Purdy (Value, +62), Jared Goff (Value, +80), Kyler Murray (+86), Jordan Love (+95)
If · a Do Not Draft player looks like unmissable value

Still don't take him

This will happen twice. TreVeyon Henderson sits on the board from Round 2 through Round 7, and Brian Thomas Jr. lasts all the way to Round 11 — both ranked well above where they're going, both squarely in the rounds where you'll be hunting for value.

That list is Evan's judgment that the ranking overstates the player. Take the next name on the fallback table instead — there's always one within a few spots.

If · we somehow have three QBs before Round 8

Stop entirely and take skill players for four rounds

Two quarterbacks start; a third is insurance, not an asset. Ignore the position for the rest of the draft — there's no fourth QB slot and the bench is only six deep.

Use Rounds 8 through 11 on the best available tagged back and receiver, alternating. MarShawn Lloyd in Round 10 is still the pick regardless of what else happened.

If · Tucker Kraft is gone before Round 9

Take Sam LaPorta, or wait for Kincaid

Sam LaPorta (Favorite + Value) is nearly always on the board through Round 7. If both are gone, Dalton Kincaid (Favorite + Huge Upside) holds to Round 10 and Juwan Johnson to Round 14. Any of them starts. Don't spend a Round 6 pick on a tight end just to feel safe.

If · someone takes Gibbs and Bijan before pick 2

Take the best tagged receiver, then rebalance

Unlikely — Chan opens with a receiver most years. If it happens, take Puka Nacua (Favorite), then treat Rounds 2 and 3 as running back rounds and push the first quarterback to Round 6.

06Rules that hold all draft

Two QBs by Round 6

Not one, not three. Both starting slots filled by the end of Round 6; the third only as late insurance.

Prefer the tagged player

When two names are close on the board, take the one marked Favorite, Huge Upside, Sleeper or Value.

Take MarShawn Lloyd in Round 10

Largest value gap on the board at +92, tagged Huge Upside, and essentially always there.

When it's close, take the running back

Receiver stays deep into the late rounds. Running back does not.

Never draft from the Do Not Draft list

All twelve are in section 04. No exceptions, however good the value looks.

No K or D/ST before Round 15

This league always waits and so do we. An earlier pick here wastes a roster spot outright.

Reading the tables. "Our rank" is Evan's board. "ADP" is ESPN's public average draft position — the two-quarterback format is why our quarterback ranks look aggressive against it. "Gap" is how many spots above market we have the player. "Likely there" is the chance he's still on the board when the pick comes around, based on how this league has drafted in past years.

One thing to keep in mind. Quarterbacks may go slightly faster than these percentages suggest. That argues for taking the Round 5 quarterback promptly rather than waiting another round.
