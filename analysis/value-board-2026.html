How to read this

"We're higher by" is how many draft slots above the market I have that player. A +64 means the field takes him 64 picks later than I'd rank him — so he'll be sitting there when we pick, and he's worth more than his draft slot suggests.

Players are listed in the last group where they're likely to still be available. That's the deadline, not the target — if you want someone, taking him a turn early is fine. Waiting past his group usually isn't.

01Positional plan

The short version: quarterback is where the edge is, running back is where the scarcity is. Spend early picks on the scarce position and cash the quarterback edge in the middle rounds.

QB32 value plays · median +52

We start two, and this is the deepest pool of value on the whole board — 16 quarterbacks sit 50+ slots above their draft cost. The market underrates them because most leagues start one.

Take both starters in Rounds 5–7. Not earlier. A third late as insurance.

RB16 value plays · median +13

The scarcest position — half as many value plays as QB or WR, and the smallest gaps. Where my board and the market mostly agree, which means you have to pay market price.

Take three in Rounds 1–3, while they exist. Don't wait.

WR32 value plays · median +22

The deepest position. Real gaps stay available past board rank 150 — there are still receivers worth +20 or more in the last few rounds.

One early (Round 4), then keep taking them from Round 7 onward.

TE4 value plays · median +17

Only one starts and there are just four tight ends I rank meaningfully above market. Outside those four it barely matters who you take.

Grab one of the four around Round 9. Don't reach earlier.

K + DSTlast two rounds

Everyone in this league waits until the end, so supply far outstrips demand at our picks. Spending anything earlier wastes a roster spot.

Rounds 15 and 16, defense first.
