# Points Left on the Bench — 2022–2025

Every team-week measured against the best lineup that roster could have started. 680 team-weeks, 68 per manager, so totals compare directly.

## Method

For each team-week the best legal lineup is solved exactly (max-weight assignment of players to starting slots), then compared to what was actually started. The gap is **points left on the bench**.

- Starting slots were 2 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, TE, FLEX, K, D/ST **plus an IDP slot in 2022–23** — dropped from 2024.
- Slot eligibility comes from the data: every starting slot a player was actually used in league-wide, plus the defaults for their position. This picks up multi-position eligibility rather than assuming it.
- **IR players are excluded** — ESPN would not have allowed them to start.
- This is measured with hindsight. It is the cost of the lineup actually set, not a claim the manager could have known.

League-wide the average team leaves **18.5 points** on the bench per week. Only 37 of 680 lineups (5.4%) were perfect.

## Caveats

- Hindsight. Benching a player who then goes off is only a mistake after the fact; this measures cost, not foreseeability.
- Optimal lineups assume the opponent still scores what they actually scored. In reality both managers were guessing.
- Eligibility is inferred from observed usage plus position defaults. A player who was never started anywhere in a slot he was technically eligible for could be slightly under-credited.
- IR players are excluded, but a player left in a normal bench slot while injured still counts as available — that is a roster-management cost the numbers include.
