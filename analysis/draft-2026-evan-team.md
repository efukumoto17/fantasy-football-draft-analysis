## The strategy was right; the execution leaked in the middle rounds

The core bet — pay up for running backs, refuse to pay up for quarterbacks — is
**supported by the actual shape of this player pool**:

| Pos | Starters needed | Best | Last starter | Drop | Drop/week |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RB | 25 | 369 | 194 | **175** | **10.3** |
| WR | 27 | 354 | 206 | 147 | 8.7 |
| QB | 20 | 370 | 264 | **106** | **6.2** |
| TE | 12 | 242 | 169 | 73 | 4.3 |

QB curve: QB1 370 → QB5 317 → QB10 292 → QB15 283 → **QB20 264**
RB curve: RB1 369 → RB5 293 → RB10 274 → RB15 259 → **RB20 210**

Even in a 2-QB league the quarterback position is the *flattest* one on the board.
The gap from the 6th QB to the 20th QB is ~36 points — barely 2 points a week —
while the RB cliff is 175 points. Nine other managers treated 2-QB as "QBs are
scarce, panic." Sixteen QBs were gone by pick 59. You let that run happen and took
Bijan (VOR 158), Walker and Saquon instead, and it bought you the best backfield in
the league.

**The counterfactual confirms it.** Taking Jalen Hurts at 19 instead of Walker
would have added 55 points at QB — but Ashton Jeanty was the best RB left at your
pick 22, so you'd have swapped Walker (273) for a similar back and given up
nothing at RB. That specific swap was roughly break-even to slightly positive.
Nothing larger was available. Waiting was not the mistake.
