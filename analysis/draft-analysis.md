# League Draft Behavior Analysis (2022–2025)

League ID 275797 · 10 managers · based on `../draft-history-combined.csv` (660 picks over 4 drafts).

> **Format note — read this first.** The league changed format partway through:
> - **2022–2023: IDP** (Individual Defensive Players) — **18 rounds**, defenders (DT/DE/LB/CB/S) drafted in the back half.
> - **2024–2025: Standard** — **16 rounds**, no IDP.
>
> Because of this, the late-round numbers are era-dependent. The tables below are split so the averages aren't misleading: a combined 4-year view, then each era on its own.

## 2. League-wide patterns

- **It's a WR-first league.** WR is the most-drafted position in Round 1 (≈4.5/round in the standard era) and dominates the early-middle rounds (Rounds 4, 8, 9 run ~5 WRs each). WR is the top position group by total picks for most managers.
- **The RB run hits in Round 2.** Round 2 is the single most RB-heavy round of the draft — about **5 of 10 picks** are RBs. Round 12 shows a second RB spike (handcuff/upside season).
- **QB clusters in Rounds 6–7.** After the elite tier, a run forms: ~4 QBs come off the board in Round 6 and again in Round 7. Managers who don't take an elite QB early nearly all pounce in this window.
- **Only two managers pay up for QB early** (Round 1): Rollin and Darwin. Everyone else waits.
- **No TE run — it's a "pick your spot" position.** TE never exceeds ~1.5–2.5 per round. A handful of managers spend a premium pick on an elite TE (Kelce, Bowers, Andrews); the rest punt to Round 10+.
- **K and D/ST are strictly end-game.** In the standard era, **D/ST floods Round 14 (~5/round)** and **K floods Round 15 (~4/round)**. Nobody reaches for either — a completely disciplined, uniform behavior across all 10 managers.
- **IDP era (2022–23):** defenders started trickling in around Round 9–10 and became the dominant pick from Round 11 onward, peaking in the final rounds (17–18 were ~40% defense).

## 3. Manager-by-manager draft strategy

Same 10 people every year (they rename teams annually — mapping in `../draft-history-team-managers.csv`). Notes cover QB timing, TE approach, RB/WR lean, and how each opens the draft.

### Rollin Odama-Wong — *The QB Anchor*
- **First-round QB in all 4 years** (Josh Allen ×2, Mahomes, Lamar Jackson). The only manager who consistently pays elite-QB prices.
- Round 2 is always a skill anchor (Bijan, Mixon, G. Wilson, Nico). Punts TE (first TE ~Round 8).
- **Read:** locks a positional weekly edge at QB, then plays best-available RB/WR. Highest-conviction, most identifiable strategy in the league.

### Darwin Hu — *Elite QB, WR volume*
- Started RB-heavy in 2022 (J. Taylor + Saquon), then converted to the **QB-in-Round-1** camp for 2023–2025 (Hurts, Allen, Allen).
- Most WR-leaning roster overall (21 WR). Ignores TE until Round 8–10.
- **Read:** a second elite-QB believer; builds a wide WR base around the QB.

### Christopher Pascual — *Reformed elite-TE, now RB anchor*
- The league's **elite-TE guy in the IDP era** — Kelce in Round 1 (2023) and Round 2 (2022).
- **Abandoned it** as Kelce aged: 2024–2025 both opened Bijan Robinson in Round 1 and pushed TE to Round 9–10.
- Earliest-QB of the "non-Round-1" group (avg ~Round 3.5).
- **Read:** adapts year to year; currently a hero-RB + early-QB build.

### Ikaika Stone — *Zero-RB lean*
- **Fewest early RBs in the league** (only 5 RBs in Rounds 1–6 across 4 years vs 8 WRs). Loads WRs and grabs an early-ish QB (avg Round 3), then backfills RB.
- Round 1 mixes anchor RB/WR (Dalvin Cook, ARSB, CeeDee) and once an elite QB (Mahomes 2023).
- **Read:** closest thing to a Zero-RB / robust-WR drafter.

### Evan Fukumoto — *WR volume, hard TE punt*
- **Most WRs drafted of anyone (22).** Opens Round 1 with an RB anchor (Ekeler, Breece, Gibbs) most years.
- **Punts TE the hardest with Harvey** — average first TE around **Round 10**, as late as Round 14–15.
- Recently moved QB up: took Lamar (2024) and Hurts (2025) in Round 2 — an early-QB pivot.
- **Read:** RB anchor → QB in Round 2 → stack WRs → stream TE.

### Harvey Wang — *Extreme TE punt, balanced skill*
- **Only 4 TEs drafted in 4 years — the fewest in the league.** First TE averages ~Round 10.5.
- Balanced RB/WR early, mid-round QB (~Round 4.5). Round 1 alternates RB and WR (Najee, JJ, Tyreek, Saquon).
- **Read:** spends nothing on TE, keeps skill-position flexibility, streams the position all season.

### Jeffrey Chan — *WR-first with an early TE tilt*
- **WR in Round 1 in 3 of 4 years** (Kupp, JJ ×2); the exception was CMC (2023).
- One of the few who'll take TE relatively early (as high as Round 3 in 2023). Drafts the **most kickers (5)** — likely a K-streamer who churns the roster spot.
- **Read:** elite-WR anchor, willing to pay for a difference-making TE.

### Justin Ho — *RB-heavy, latest QB*
- **Most RB-leaning early drafter** (10 RBs vs 8 WRs in Rounds 1–6). Frequently doubles RB in Rounds 1–2 (CMC + Etienne in 2024).
- **Waits longest on QB alongside Chase** (avg ~Round 6). Grabbed Mark Andrews in Round 2 (2022) as an early-TE exception.
- **Read:** anchor-RB / hero-RB build, streams QB late.

### Prashanth Balaraman — *WR-first, Chase superfan*
- **Drafted Ja'Marr Chase in Round 1 three times** (2022, 2023, 2024). WR in Round 1 in 3 of 4 years.
- Balanced RB/WR afterward, mid-round QB (~Round 5), moderate TE (moved it up to Round 3–4 recently).
- **Read:** elite-WR anchor with a strong personal-favorite bias toward Chase.

### Chase Mizoguchi — *Latest QB, WR volume, opportunistic TE*
- **Latest average first-QB in the league (~Round 6).** Most WR-leaning of anyone in the early rounds (12 WRs vs 8 RBs in Rounds 1–6).
- Evolved from RB anchors (CMC, Ekeler in 2022–23) to elite WRs (CeeDee, Chase in 2024–25), and pounced on **Brock Bowers in Round 2 (2025)**.
- **Read:** best-player-available WR-heavy build; will take a premium TE if it falls, never reaches for QB.
